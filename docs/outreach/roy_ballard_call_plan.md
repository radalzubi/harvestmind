# Roy Ballard / Warm Connector Call Plan

For any call with a connector — Roy Ballard, a Purdue extension agent, a
Regional Food Business Center contact, or a DIAL Ventures connection — who
already knows the regional produce operator landscape and can make one
introduction.

The connector is not the customer. The connector is the gatekeeper to
the first customer. Treat the call accordingly.

---

## Objective of the call

Get one specific introduction. Specifically: the name, role, and contact
method for one regional produce operator who:

- Aggregates from multiple farms
- Sells to institutional buyers (schools, hospitals, universities, foodservice)
- Runs ops on QuickBooks plus spreadsheets (not on a full ERP)
- Has revenue between $1M and $25M
- Is large enough to feel waste and missed-order pain in dollars
- Has someone in a GM or operations-lead role who answers email

Secondary objective: enough context on the segment to tailor outreach to
that operator and the next ten.

If the call ends without a specific name or a clear "let me think about who
fits and email you", the call was a failure even if the conversation was
pleasant.

---

## One-sentence pitch

"We've built a working diagnostic tool that takes a produce hub's existing
CSV exports and produces a board-readable report on waste loss, shortage
loss, supplier reliability, and FSMA-204 readiness — and we're looking
for one real operator with messy data who'd be willing to share 6–12 months
of it for a paid diagnostic."

That sentence is the entire pitch. Do not improvise a longer version.

---

## What not to say

- "We're building an AI platform for agriculture"
- "We use stochastic optimization to..." (the connector does not care)
- "We're going to disrupt..." (the connector mentally checks out)
- "It's free" (you need a buyer, not a recipient)
- "We don't have customers yet but..." (lead with the working MVP, not the gap)
- Anything that makes the connector responsible for your success
- A multi-paragraph product description before they've asked

---

## Eight questions to ask

Listen for proper-noun answers. Names, organizations, regions. If they answer
in generalities, gently re-ask with a specific frame.

1. **"When you think about regional produce operators who'd be a good fit for
   a 30-day paid diagnostic — who comes to mind first?"**
   (This is the only question that actually matters. Ask it early.)

2. "Of the hubs you work with, which ones are big enough to feel waste in
   real dollars but not big enough to have ERP and a data team?"

3. "Which operators are you aware of who are facing FSMA-204 pressure from a
   buyer right now? Sysco, school districts, hospital networks?"

4. "What's the language an operator in this segment uses when they describe
   the problem we just walked through? Do they say 'waste,' 'shrink,'
   'overcommit,' 'spoilage,' something else?"

5. "When a vendor approaches a regional hub for the first time, what's the
   single thing that gets them taken seriously vs ignored?"

6. "Who else in your network — extension agents, regional center staff,
   industry consultants — would have a different angle on this segment?"

7. "If you were running this outreach, what would you change about how
   we're framing the diagnostic?"

8. "Is there anyone we should specifically avoid approaching this early —
   operators who'd be a poor first customer for reasons that aren't
   obvious from outside?"

Questions 1, 4, and 8 are the highest signal. Make sure all three are asked
even if the call runs short.

---

## Exact intro ask

Use this script verbatim:

> "Roy, the most useful thing for us at this stage is a single warm
> introduction to one produce operator who'd be willing to share 6 to 12
> months of messy operational data for a structured diagnostic. We can pay
> for the engagement so it's not a favor ask on their side — but we need a
> first customer who trusts the person introducing them.
>
> If a name comes to mind today, we'd love to hear it. If you'd like to
> think about it for a few days, I'll send you a one-page memo by tomorrow
> morning and follow up at the end of the week. Which works better for you?"

That phrasing does three things at once: makes the ask specific, removes
the "I'm being asked for a favor for free" friction, and gives a clear
follow-up commitment.

## Ideal operator profile (share verbally if asked)

| Attribute | Ideal | Acceptable | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3M–$15M | $1M–$25M | < $1M or > $50M |
| Farm count aggregated | 10–40 | 5–60 | < 3 or > 80 |
| Institutional buyer mix | Schools, hospitals, foodservice | Plus some grocery | All wholesale-only |
| Tech stack | QuickBooks + Excel | Adds Local Line or similar | Full ERP (NetSuite, Famous) |
| FSMA exposure | Buyer asking for traceability | Buyer might ask soon | Already fully compliant |
| Operator personality | Operator-led, data-curious | Curious-but-busy | "Spreadsheet is fine" purist |
| Location | Midwest preferred (Purdue proximity) | National | International (too complex for first pilot) |

## After the call — what to do regardless of outcome

- Update the prospects sheet within 1 hour with everything you learned
- If they mentioned a specific operator: research that operator within 24
  hours so you're prepared when the intro arrives
- If they mentioned a peer connector: add to the prospects sheet and queue
  for outreach in 1–2 weeks
- If they offered to forward the memo: send the memo within 24 hours and
  follow up at day 7
- Always send a thank-you note even if no intro materialized

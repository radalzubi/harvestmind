# Roy Ballard / Warm Connector Call Plan

For any call with a connector — Roy Ballard, a Purdue extension agent, a
Regional Food Business Center contact, or a DIAL Ventures connection — who
already knows the produce operator landscape and can make introductions.

The connector is not the customer. The connector is the gatekeeper to the
first paid diagnostic. Treat the call accordingly.

---

## Objective of the call

1. **Primary** — Get one specific introduction to a produce operator
   willing to share 6–12 months of messy operational data for a structured
   paid diagnostic.
2. **Secondary** — Get the connector's read on which of three business
   shapes (SaaS, productized diagnostic, ERP add-on) the segment is most
   likely to support, and which operator categories are most worth
   approaching.

If the call ends without (a) a specific name or (b) a clear "let me think
about who fits and email you", the call was a failure even if the
conversation was pleasant.

---

## One-sentence pitch

"HarvestMind is a diagnostic engine for perishable produce operators. It
uses CSV exports from existing systems — spreadsheets, ERPs, inventory
tools, or order platforms — to estimate waste loss, shortage loss,
supplier reliability, buyer fulfillment risk, traceability gaps, and
available-to-promise recommendations. We're running the first 3–5 paid
diagnostics on real operator data to find out whether the strongest
business model is SaaS, productized consulting, or an ERP add-on."

That paragraph is the entire pitch. Do not improvise a longer version.

---

## What not to say

- "We're building an AI platform for agriculture"
- "We use stochastic optimization to..." (the connector does not care)
- "We're going to win the food hub market" (we don't know that and shouldn't claim it)
- "It's free" (you need a buyer, not a recipient)
- "We've validated this with [N] operators" (we haven't)
- "We'll save them $X" (we don't have customer-validated savings)
- Anything that makes the connector responsible for your success
- A multi-paragraph product description before they've asked

---

## Eight questions to ask

Listen for proper-noun answers. Names, organizations, regions. If they
answer in generalities, gently re-ask with a specific frame.

1. **"When you think about produce operators who'd be a good fit for a
   paid 30-day diagnostic — who comes to mind first?"**
   *(Highest-signal question. Ask early.)*

2. "Of the operators you work with, which ones are big enough to feel
   waste in real dollars but not so locked into an ERP that adding a new
   tool is impossible?"

3. "When you talk to operators about FSMA-204, are they more worried
   about waste, shortage, or traceability? Which pain leads?"

4. "What's the language an operator in this segment uses when they
   describe the problem we just walked through? 'Waste'? 'Shrink'?
   'Overcommit'? 'Spoilage'? Something else?"

5. "When a new vendor approaches a produce operator for the first time,
   what's the single thing that gets them taken seriously vs. ignored?"

6. "Of three product shapes — one-time paid diagnostic, recurring
   quarterly diagnostic, or live software subscription — which do you
   think this segment would be most willing to actually buy?"

7. "Who else in your network — extension agents, regional center staff,
   industry consultants — would have a different angle on this segment?"

8. "Is there any operator we should specifically avoid approaching early
   — operators who'd be a poor first customer for reasons not obvious
   from outside?"

Questions 1, 4, 6, and 8 are the highest signal. Make sure all four are
asked even if the call runs short.

---

## Exact intro ask

Use this script verbatim:

> "Roy, the most useful thing for us at this stage is a single warm
> introduction to one produce operator who'd be willing to share 6 to 12
> months of messy operational data for a structured paid diagnostic. We
> can pay them for the engagement so it's not a favor ask on their side
> — but we need a first customer who trusts the person introducing them.
>
> If a name comes to mind today, we'd love to hear it. If you'd like to
> think about it for a few days, I'll send you a one-page memo by
> tomorrow morning and follow up at the end of the week. Which works
> better for you?"

That phrasing does three things at once: makes the ask specific, removes
the "I'm being asked for a favor for free" friction, and gives a clear
follow-up commitment.

## Ideal operator profile (share verbally if asked)

We are open to a wide range of operator profiles because the first paid
diagnostics are meant to reveal which segment is the strongest pull.

| Attribute | Ideal | Acceptable | Avoid for first pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3M–$15M | $1M–$50M | <$1M (too small) |
| Farm count aggregated | 10–40 | 5–60 | <3 (not a hub) |
| Institutional buyer mix | Schools, hospitals, foodservice | Plus some grocery | All wholesale-only consumer-facing |
| Tech stack | QuickBooks + Excel | Any (incl. ERP) | Cutting-edge full enterprise stack already optimized |
| FSMA exposure | Buyer asking for traceability | Buyer might ask soon | Already fully compliant via vendor |
| Operator personality | Operator-led, data-curious | Curious-but-busy | "Spreadsheet is fine and always will be" |
| Location | Midwest preferred (Purdue proximity) | National | International (too complex for first pilot) |

The key shift from earlier framing: **we are no longer screening out
ERP-equipped operators**. If a NetSuite/Famous-running operator wants the
diagnostic, that's actually one of the most valuable data points we can
collect — it tells us whether Path C (ERP add-on) is real.

## After the call — what to do regardless of outcome

- Update the prospects sheet within 1 hour with everything you learned
- If they mentioned a specific operator: research that operator within
  24 hours so you're prepared when the intro arrives. Note their tech
  stack — it's the most important variable for interpreting their
  feedback later.
- If they mentioned a peer connector: add to the prospects sheet and
  queue for outreach in 1–2 weeks
- If they offered to forward the memo: send the memo within 24 hours and
  follow up at day 7
- Always send a thank-you note even if no intro materialized

## Tracking which path the connector thinks is real

In your call notes, add a single field: **"Which path did the connector
seem to lean toward?"** with options:

- A (SaaS for spreadsheet-heavy hubs)
- B (Productized diagnostic / consulting)
- C (ERP add-on for ERP-equipped operators)
- Couldn't tell

Across 5–10 connector calls, the distribution of those answers is itself
useful signal — it tells you which path your network believes in before
the operators do.

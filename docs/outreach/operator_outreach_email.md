# Operator Outreach Emails

Two short emails to produce operators (food hubs, aggregators, wholesalers,
ERP-equipped distributors — the diagnostic works for any of them).

**Goal**: Get a 25-minute discovery call. Eventually: one real dataset and
one paid diagnostic.

**Do not** lead with AI, optimization, market size, or Purdue research. Lead
with the operational pain the GM already feels but hasn't quantified.
**Do not** claim recurring savings, customer references, or business-model
certainty — none of those exist yet.

---

## Version A — Warm intro (someone the operator trusts has made the connection)

**Subject**: Following [Connector]'s intro — quick diagnostic question

Hi [First name],

[Connector first name] mentioned you'd be a good person to talk to. I'll
keep this short.

I work on a tool called HarvestMind. It takes the CSV exports a produce
operator already pulls out of QuickBooks, an ERP, or operations
spreadsheets, and turns them into a board-readable report on:

- Where you're losing money to waste and near-expiry stock
- Which suppliers are quietly under-shipping and what it costs in missed orders
- Which buyers we keep failing to fulfill, and the dollar impact
- FSMA-204 lot traceability — what percentage of lots have all the records
  a buyer would ask for in an audit

It's a 30-day paid diagnostic. No integration. No IT project. You export
the CSVs, we run the analysis, you get the report.

Two questions for a 25-minute call:

1. Does any of the above match a problem you've been carrying in your head?
2. If we ran this against your actual data, would the dollar figures be
   useful to your GM or CFO?

The honest framing: we are at the pre-customer-validation stage. The MVP
works on sample data, and we're looking for the first 3–5 operators to run
the diagnostic against real data so we can find out which version of the
product — one-time analysis, recurring quarterly, or live tool — actually
makes sense for operators like you.

I have time [day 1] morning, [day 2] afternoon, or anything that fits your
week.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone number]

## Version B — Cold outbound (no introduction, found via USDA/grant directories)

**Subject**: Quick question about [Operator name]'s waste tracking

Hi [First name],

I'm building a diagnostic tool for produce operators and I'm reaching out
because [one specific reason — they're a Regional Food Business Center
partner / they were a 2024 LFPP awardee / they're listed as supplying
[buyer]]. That tells me your operation aggregates and ships perishables
to institutional buyers, which is exactly the shape of operation that
tends to leak margin to waste, missed orders, and substitution patterns
most teams don't see in dollars.

I'm not selling an ERP and not selling software you have to integrate.
The tool takes the CSV exports you already pull out of QuickBooks, your
ERP, or your operations spreadsheets, and produces a report covering:

- Waste loss in dollars, by product and reason
- Missed-order loss and which buyers are most affected
- Supplier fill rate and rejection rate
- Inventory near-expiry exposure
- FSMA-204 lot traceability completeness

Honest framing: this is a 30-day paid diagnostic, not a long-term software
commitment. We're at the pre-customer-validation stage and are running the
first paid diagnostics specifically to find out which version of the
analysis — one-time report, recurring engagement, or live tool — operators
in your category actually find useful.

Would you have 25 minutes in the next two weeks for a no-commitment call?
I'd like to show you what the report looks like on a sample dataset and
get your honest read on whether the framing matches your reality.

If now isn't a good time, no offense taken — happy to circle back next
quarter.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone number]

## Subject line variants worth A/B-ing

- "Where [Operator name]'s margin is probably going"
- "FSMA-204 question for [Operator name]"
- "30-day diagnostic for [Operator name] — worth 25 minutes?"
- "Quick question about your [most distinctive product line]"

---

## What to send with the email

**Nothing.** No deck attachment. No PDF. No link to a generic landing page.

The point of the email is to earn the 25-minute call. The demo is the demo.
If pushed for "more info first", reply with a one-paragraph version of the
problem and a single sentence about the diagnostic — never a deck dump.

---

## What to do if they don't respond

- **Day 0**: First email sent
- **Day 7**: Single-line follow-up — "Following up on the note below — open
  to a 25-minute call in the next two weeks?"
- **Day 21**: Final note — "Closing the loop. If timing isn't right today
  I'd love to circle back in Q[n]. Mind if I check in then?"
- **No fourth email.** Move to the next prospect and revisit after one
  full season has passed.

---

## What never to put in operator outreach

- The word "AI" or "artificial intelligence"
- The word "platform"
- The phrase "data-driven decision making"
- A claim about customer count, revenue, or funding (you have none)
- A claim about specific dollar savings ("you could save $X/yr") — that's
  not something we can support without operator data
- Anything about Purdue research, optimization theory, or PhD-style methodology
- A free pilot offer ("free" attracts the wrong operator)
- A long signature with badges or social links

---

## How to handle "how much will we save?" on a discovery call

The honest answer:

> "I don't know yet. The diagnostic engine quantifies waste, shortage,
> supplier reliability, fulfillment risk, and traceability gaps from
> your actual data. Industry research (USDA SR-77) suggests operators
> who act on this kind of analysis typically recover 15–30% of identified
> addressable loss. Whether that figure is meaningful for your operation
> specifically is exactly what running the diagnostic on your data would
> tell us. We've not yet validated it against real operator datasets —
> you would be in the first cohort."

That answer beats a guess every time. Operators trust honesty more than
optimism in a first conversation.

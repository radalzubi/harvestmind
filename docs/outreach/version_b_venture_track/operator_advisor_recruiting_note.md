# Operator-Side Advisor / Co-Founder Recruiting Note

_Internal planning document. Not for outreach. Outlines who to look
for, why this role is the single highest-leverage credibility move,
what they would do in the first 60 days, and how the advisor vs.
co-founder distinction should be made before any equity is
discussed._

---

## Why this role matters

Investors at the seed stage and beyond consistently apply the same
filter to first-time founders in agrifood: **does the team have
operator-side credibility?**

A Purdue student team building a perishable-produce diagnostic, no
matter how strong the product, will not credibly raise a venture
round without at least one team member or formal advisor who can
answer the question:

> *"Have you actually run a produce distribution operation, and if
> so, did your operators behave the way HarvestMind's diagnostic
> assumes they do?"*

The answer is currently no. Until that gap is filled, every
investor pitch will be discounted, and rightly so.

This is not a marketing problem. It is a credibility-of-claims
problem. Operators who pressure-test the diagnostic find subtle
mistakes that someone who hasn't worked in produce wouldn't catch.
Examples from the math audit we just completed:

- Should "fulfillment rate" count substituted orders as fulfilled
  or not? Depends on buyer contract terms an outsider doesn't know.
- What's the right near-expiry threshold? Three days for leafy
  greens; could be 14 days for citrus. An operator knows; we
  guessed.
- Does $1.50/kg fallback pricing for waste valuation make sense?
  An operator would have said "use cost-basis, not retail" the
  moment they saw it. We caught it on audit; an operator would
  have caught it on first read.

An operator-side advisor catches these before they reach
operator demos. That is what the role is for.

## Ideal candidate profile

| Attribute | Ideal | Acceptable | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Industry experience** | 10+ years in produce distribution, food hub, foodservice produce division, or grocery DC produce | 5–10 years | Pure CPG / packaged-food background — doesn't translate |
| **Functional background** | GM, VP Operations, COO, or Sales Director who owned commit/fulfillment decisions | Inventory manager, buyer, or sales rep | Pure finance or pure tech roles |
| **Operator network** | Active relationships with 10+ peer operators they could introduce | 3–10 peer relationships | No active network |
| **Tech familiarity** | Has used or evaluated produce ERPs (Famous, Produce Pro, Silo), or made buying decisions about them | Familiar with the names | "I don't do software" |
| **Career stage** | Mid-career, currently consulting or recently transitioned. Has time. | Active operator willing to advise on the side | Founder of a competing startup |
| **Geographic accessibility** | Midwest, or willing to do quarterly in-person | National with quarterly travel | International only |
| **Skin in the game** | Wants equity, not cash | Mix of cash + equity | Cash only |

The realistic catch: someone with all of these attributes is also a
strong candidate for COO at a real produce distributor making
$300K+/yr. The pitch to them is **upside ownership**, not salary.

## Where to find candidates

1. **LinkedIn search**: "VP Operations" or "Director of Operations"
   at named produce distributors and food hubs. Filter for "Open
   to work" or recent transitions.
2. **Regional Food Business Centers**: Some staff are former
   operators who transitioned to extension/program roles.
3. **Industry associations**: PMA / IFPA conferences, United Fresh
   member directory, National Grocers Association.
4. **Wallace Center alumni**: Their food hub manager training
   program has produced 100+ operator-side professionals over the
   years.
5. **Purdue alumni network**: agricultural economics + ag systems
   management majors who went into produce distribution roles.
6. **Referrals from Roy Ballard / extension agents**: They know
   the people who would do this well.
7. **Former employees of larger produce distributors**: People who
   ran specific regions at Sysco/US Foods/PFG produce divisions
   and have either retired or transitioned.

## First-meeting framing

The first conversation is **not** "would you be our co-founder."
That's a top-of-funnel approach guaranteed to fail. The right
first conversation is:

> "We built a diagnostic engine for produce operators. Before we
> approach real operators, we'd value your operational read on
> whether the diagnostic asks the right questions and produces
> defensible output. Would you spend 60 minutes critiquing the
> sample-data version of the report and pointing out where it
> would feel naive to a working operator?"

That ask gets a yes from candidates who would never say yes to a
co-founder pitch. After 1–2 sessions like that, the conversation
naturally moves to "would you like to be more involved" — and at
that point the answer is informed by real shared work, not by a
recruiter's pitch.

## First 60 days of involvement

Once a candidate has agreed to engage at advisor level (whether
formally or informally), here is the work plan:

| Week | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walkthrough of the diagnostic on sample data with full Q&A | List of "things an operator would notice" — likely 15–30 items |
| 2 | Review of the customer intake docs (`docs/customer_intake/`) for operator-facing realism | Edits to field definitions and data request checklist |
| 3 | Review of the pilot offer (`docs/outreach/pilot_offer.md`) for operator-side credibility | Edits to scope, pricing, and language |
| 4 | Review of the 5-minute demo script for "things an operator would push back on" | Edits to demo language |
| 5–6 | Introduction to 1–2 peer operators they trust, for first paid diagnostic conversations | Warm intros |
| 7–8 | Co-presence on the first operator discovery call | Joint readout afterwards |

If at week 8 the candidate is energized and engaged, that is the
right time to discuss whether the relationship should become
formal advisor or co-founder. If they have lost interest, that
is also useful signal — it likely means we aren't ready for them
yet.

## Advisor vs. co-founder — the distinction

| Dimension | Advisor | Co-founder |
|---|---|---|
| Time commitment | 2–8 hours / month | 20+ hours / week, eventually full-time |
| Equity range | 0.10% – 1.50% vesting over 2 years | 5% – 25% vesting over 4 years with 1-year cliff |
| Decision authority | Voice in product/strategy, no vote on hiring/equity | Material vote on company-wide decisions |
| Compensation | Equity-only typical | Equity + delayed salary; cash when company can afford |
| Exit involvement | Helps with intros, advisor capacity through any exit | Material role through any exit |
| When formalized | After 60–90 days of working together | After 90–180 days of working together |
| Replaceable? | Yes, with low cost to company | No, replacing a co-founder is a near-fatal event |

**Bias the conversation toward advisor first, co-founder later.**
Almost every founder who locked in a co-founder too fast regrets
it. Almost no founder regrets bringing on a strong advisor early
and converting them to co-founder later. The reverse path is
rarely available.

## Equity ranges (after legal / IP / COI review only)

These are starting points, not commitments. Apply only after:

1. **Purdue Innovates and OTC have cleared** any IP question
2. **Any advisor's employer COI process** has been completed
   if they are still active in industry
3. **A signed advisor agreement** is in hand (use FAST template
   or equivalent reviewed by counsel)
4. **At least 60 days of working relationship** has happened

| Engagement type | Typical equity (FAST equivalent) | Vesting |
|---|---|---|
| Light advisor (≤ 2 hours/month) | 0.10% – 0.25% | 2 yrs, monthly, no cliff |
| Active advisor (3–8 hours/month, intros, co-meetings) | 0.25% – 0.75% | 2 yrs, monthly, no cliff or 3-month cliff |
| Strategic advisor (8+ hours/month, deep involvement) | 0.75% – 1.50% | 2 yrs, monthly, 3-month cliff |
| Co-founder (full-time eventually) | 5% – 25% (varies with timing and contribution) | 4 yrs, monthly, 1-year cliff |

**Critical rule**: Do not name a number, even informally, before
both COI and IP clearance. Verbal equity commitments can create
disputes that destroy relationships you would rather keep.

the full equity process notes that apply to all Purdue-affiliated
advisors, including operator-side ones who happen to be Purdue
alumni.

## Red flags to walk away from

| Signal | What it likely means |
|---|---|
| Candidate asks for equity in the first conversation | Transactional posture; relationship will not be load-bearing |
| Candidate cannot describe a specific operator they would introduce | Their network claim is thin |
| Candidate wants only cash, no equity | They don't believe in the outcome; do not engage further |
| Candidate has a non-compete from a recent operator employer | Legal risk; either resolve cleanly or walk |
| Candidate disparages every other tool they've used | Likely to disparage HarvestMind to operators too |
| Candidate is currently CEO/COO at a direct competitor | Conflict of interest; walk |
| Candidate wants to bring on additional people from their previous company | Team-formation risk; revisit only after company structure is mature |

## What good candidates look like in conversation

Conversely, a strong candidate typically:

- Asks specific operational questions ("does the fill-rate
  calculation handle substitution-by-grade?")
- Names 3–5 operators by name within the first hour, unprompted,
  who they think might be a fit
- Pushes back on at least one assumption in the diagnostic engine
  during the first review — and is right
- Has worked in operations long enough to know what won't work,
  not just what should
- Is comfortable saying "I don't know" about parts of the business
  they haven't owned
- Wants to be paid in upside, not in cash flow

Track every candidate against this list. If 4+ of the indicators
are true in the first conversation, advance them.

## The single most important thing

Get this hire right and the venture-track conversation becomes
substantially more credible. Get it wrong and you spend two years
recovering. Treat it accordingly — slow, deliberate, evidence-based.

If at month 6 of operations no operator-side advisor has formalized,
that is meaningful negative signal about the venture thesis. It
likely means either (a) we are pitching the role wrong, or (b) the
problem isn't compelling enough to operator-side professionals to
trade career stability for upside. Either finding is worth knowing.

# 12-Month Venture Path

_A realistic month-by-month plan for proving (or disproving) the
venture-track thesis, with explicit kill/pivot criteria at every
checkpoint._

The point of this document is to commit, in advance, to honest
decision points. At months 3, 6, 9, and 12 we either advance the
venture-track thesis or we pivot. The criteria are written down
**before** the data comes in, so we cannot rationalize them
afterward.

## Months 1–3 — First Paid Diagnostics

### Goals

1. Send the 5 professor outreach emails (Version A)
2. Send DIAL Ventures email (Version B)
3. Send Purdue Innovates email (Version B)
4. Begin operator-side advisor candidate outreach (3–5 candidates)
5. **Run 3 paid diagnostics on real operator data**

### Product milestones
- One operator's real CSV exports loaded into the diagnostic
- One diagnostic report customized for an operator (not sample data)
- Any data-shape bugs surfaced and fixed
- Customer intake docs updated based on first real data load

### Customer milestones
- 3 paid pilots signed at $3K–$15K each
- 5+ operator discovery calls held (not all convert)
- 1+ operator confirms findings are directionally accurate in writing

### Team milestones
- 5+ professor meetings completed
- 1 DIAL Ventures meeting completed
- 1+ operator-side advisor candidate in active engagement
- Purdue Innovates process initiated (IP / COI clearance question
  in flight)

### Funding milestones
- USDA SBIR Phase I application started (or decision not to pursue
  based on time-to-money math — Phase I awards typically 6+
  months from application to funding)
- No outside venture money raised
- Pilot revenue covers some operating costs

### Checkpoint at end of Month 3 — Decision criteria

**Continue venture-track thesis if at least 3 of these are true:**
- ≥ 2 paid diagnostics signed
- ≥ 1 operator confirmed findings are directionally accurate
- 1 ERP-equipped operator engaged (Path C signal)
- ≥ 1 operator-side advisor candidate has engaged seriously
- ≥ 1 venture-track advisor (DIAL or other) is willing to take a
  second meeting

**Pivot to Path B (productized diagnostic services only) if:**
- 1–2 paid diagnostics signed but no Path C signal
- No operator-side advisor engaging
- Venture-track conversations consistently cold

**Stop entirely if:**
- 0 paid diagnostics after 3 months of focused outreach
- Operators consistently say "yes interesting but no budget"
- Data export from real operators consistently fails

## Months 4–6 — Recurring Conversion

### Goals

1. Convert at least 1 pilot operator to a recurring engagement
2. Identify which of Paths A/B/C the market is pulling toward
3. Formalize at least one operator-side advisor relationship
4. Complete the remaining 2 paid diagnostics from the Q1 cohort
5. Begin a second cohort of 3 paid pilots (months 4–6)

### Product milestones
- One operator using HarvestMind output for at least one
  decision per week (Path A indicator), OR
- One operator signed up for quarterly recurring diagnostic
  (Path B indicator), OR
- One ERP-equipped operator actively scoping a Path C engagement
- ATP recommendations module validated by at least one
  operator's real-world commit decisions
- Any product gaps identified in first 3 pilots fixed

### Customer milestones
- ≥ 1 recurring engagement signed ($10K+/yr)
- ≥ 3 more paid pilots signed (total 6 by month 6)
- ≥ 1 operator reference willing to take an investor call
- Sales process post-mortem written; ICP refined

### Team milestones
- 1 formal operator-side advisor agreement signed (after
  Purdue COI/IP clearance and 60+ days of working relationship)
- DIAL Ventures second meeting (if Path C signal exists)
- 1+ peer founder conversation with a comparable agrifood
  founder

### Funding milestones
- If SBIR Phase I application was submitted, results pending
- Pre-seed conversations exploratory only
- No formal raise yet — too early

### Checkpoint at end of Month 6 — Decision criteria

**Continue venture-track thesis if all of these are true:**
- ≥ 6 paid pilots completed or in progress
- ≥ 1 recurring engagement signed
- ≥ 1 formal operator-side advisor on board
- Either Path A or Path C signal is concrete (in writing from operator)
- DIAL or another venture-track advisor says "we'd consider you
  seriously at the next stage"

**Pivot to Path B-only (productized diagnostic business) if:**
- 6+ pilots completed but ZERO recurring conversions
- All operators say "valuable but one-time"
- No Path C signal across any ERP-equipped operator

**Pause and re-evaluate if:**
- < 4 paid pilots completed
- No operator-side advisor engagement
- Founder team is at < 50% confidence

## Months 7–9 — Build the Demanded Workflow

### Goals

1. Build the version of HarvestMind that the market is pulling toward
2. Continue diagnostic engagements but use month 6's data to commit
   to one of Path A / B / C as the primary direction
3. Begin formal seed-round preparation if signals are positive

### Product milestones

**If Path A (SaaS for spreadsheet-heavy operators):**
- Live dashboard with weekly data refresh capability
- Email/Slack alerts for at-risk products
- Mobile-friendly view for sales reps
- First integration with QuickBooks Online or Local Line

**If Path B (productized recurring diagnostic):**
- Productized report generation (faster turnaround per engagement)
- Customer portal for self-service report viewing
- Standardized data-quality scoring system
- Less product investment; more sales investment

**If Path C (ATP add-on for ERP-equipped operators):**
- First integration with one produce ERP (Famous, Produce Pro,
  or similar — likely the one the design-partner operator uses)
- Real-time ATP recommendations API
- Audit trail / logging suitable for buyer review

### Customer milestones
- 3+ recurring engagements live
- Total 10+ paid pilots completed since launch
- 1+ design partner operator engaged in Path C scoping
- Customer success process documented; first quarterly
  business review with a recurring customer

### Team milestones
- First engineering hire (full-time or contract) to build the
  demanded workflow surface
- Sales process for the chosen path documented
- 2+ formal operator-side advisors

### Funding milestones
- Seed deck (Version B) finalized
- 5–10 target investor list built
- 2–3 warm intros to seed investors active

### Checkpoint at end of Month 9 — Decision criteria

**Move toward seed round if all true:**
- 10+ paid customers (mix of pilots + recurring)
- 3+ recurring engagements totaling $30K+/yr in committed revenue
- 2+ operator references willing to take investor calls
- 1+ Path C engagement closed or scoped
- Strong operator-side advisor or co-founder secured

**Bootstrap if:**
- < 5 paid customers despite focused effort
- No recurring engagements
- Founder team is comfortable building a profitable services
  business at modest scale

**Wind down if:**
- < 3 paid customers
- No operator advisor engagement
- No clear product-market signal

## Months 10–12 — Raise or Bootstrap Decision

### Goals

This is the explicit decision quarter. The next 3 months commit to
either a venture-track seed raise or to a bootstrap path. Both
are legitimate outcomes; the goal is to commit to one based on
the evidence, not to drift.

### Product milestones
- Whichever path was chosen at month 9 has been built to a level
  where new customers can be onboarded with reasonable effort
- Documentation, onboarding, and customer success processes are
  in place for the chosen path
- At least one product extension (adjacent vertical adapter,
  second integration, or significant workflow improvement)
  shipped

### Customer milestones
- 15+ paid customers
- $50K–$150K in committed annual recurring revenue
- 5+ recurring engagements
- First customer in an adjacent vertical (dairy, seafood, etc.)
  OR an ERP-equipped operator in production

### Team milestones
- Operator-side advisor or co-founder fully integrated
- First or second engineering hire onboarded
- First sales/customer success hire if Path A or Path C

### Funding milestones — DECISION POINT

**Path 1 — Venture raise:**
- $1.5M–$3M seed round closed at $8M–$15M post-money
- Lead investor: DIAL Ventures, agrifood-focused fund, or
  high-conviction angel syndicate
- Use of funds: 18 months of runway, 2–3 engineering hires,
  1 sales hire
- This path requires the venture-readiness scorecard to show
  ≥ 7 Greens

**Path 2 — Continued bootstrap:**
- Reach $250K+ ARR by month 18 without outside capital
- Reinvest revenue into product and 1 part-time hire
- Apply for SBIR Phase II if Phase I was awarded
- Revisit venture raise at month 18–24 with stronger metrics
- This path is appropriate if scorecard shows 4–6 Greens and
  the founder team prefers ownership preservation over
  capital injection

**Path 3 — Wind down:**
- 12 months of effort produced < $50K ARR and no path to
  scale
- Make the call. Write up learnings. Open-source what makes
  sense. Move on with clean exit.

### Final 12-month checkpoint — Decision criteria

The decision at month 12 is **not** a gut call. It is the
average of these signals:

| Signal | Path 1 (raise) | Path 2 (bootstrap) | Path 3 (wind down) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARR | $80K+ | $30K–$80K | < $30K |
| Recurring customers | 5+ | 2–5 | 0–2 |
| Net revenue retention | > 100% | 80–100% | < 80% |
| Pipeline | 10+ qualified | 5–10 qualified | < 5 qualified |
| Operator advisor | Formal, engaged | Formal or active | Not committed |
| Path A/B/C clarity | One path clearly winning | Mixed signals | All paths weak |
| Founder energy | High, focused | Moderate, sustainable | Burned out or distracted |

## Kill / Pivot Criteria — When to Stop

Across the 12-month plan, these are the "we are wrong about
something fundamental and need to stop or pivot" criteria:

| Signal | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Month 3: zero paid pilots despite focused outreach | Wedge product is not buyable; rethink offer or kill |
| Month 6: 0 recurring conversions from 6+ pilots | Operators value diagnostics but not ongoing engagement; pivot to Path B-only or kill venture thesis |
| Month 9: no operator-side advisor formalized | Team credibility gap will not close; either accept ceiling at smaller business or kill venture thesis |
| Month 12: < $50K ARR | Wind down or pivot to non-software model |
| Any month: founder team confidence drops below 50% for 30+ days | Pause and re-evaluate; do not push through |

---

## What this document is NOT

- **Not a prediction.** No founder has ever hit a 12-month plan
  exactly. The point is to have explicit checkpoints.
- **Not a commitment to venture-scale.** Months 9–12 explicitly
  acknowledge bootstrap as a legitimate outcome.
- **Not a pitch.** This is an internal document. Some elements
  (months 7–9 product scope, funding goals) would be pitched
  externally only with appropriate framing.
- **Not static.** Update after every milestone. The version
  history of this file is part of the learning record.

## Re-evaluation cadence

| When | What |
|---|---|
| End of each month | Did this month's milestones happen? Note delta. |
| End of each quarter | Run the venture-readiness scorecard. Compare to plan. |
| End of month 6 | Major checkpoint — Path A/B/C decision |
| End of month 12 | Final venture vs. bootstrap decision |
| Any time the founder team is < 50% confident for 30+ days | Pause and re-evaluate; do not push through |

## What good looks like at month 12

If everything goes well, here is the version of HarvestMind that
emerges at month 12:

- 15+ paid customers spread across at least 2 of the 3 paths
- $80K–$150K ARR
- 5+ recurring customers
- 1 formal operator-side co-founder or strong advisor
- 1 engineering hire onboarded
- $1.5M–$3M seed round closed at $10M–$15M post-money
- Lead investor: DIAL Ventures or comparable agrifood-focused fund
- Use of funds: 18 months runway to $1M ARR and Series A position

That outcome is achievable but **not guaranteed**. The plan above
is the work that has to happen for it to be possible. None of it
is shortcuts.

## What honest looks like at month 12

There is also a perfectly respectable outcome where:

- 5–10 paid customers, mostly one-time engagements
- $50K–$100K annual revenue (mostly services)
- No outside capital raised
- Operator-side advisor relationship formed
- Founders run the business profitably as a 2-person shop

That is **not** a venture outcome. It is a real, valuable,
durable small business in the agrifood services space. If the
data points to that outcome, take it. Forcing venture economics
on a business that doesn't support them is the most common way
agrifood startups destroy themselves.

The discipline of this document is to commit, in advance, to
respecting whichever outcome the evidence supports.

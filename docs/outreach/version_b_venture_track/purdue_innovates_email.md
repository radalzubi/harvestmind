# Purdue Innovates — Outreach Email

_Single tailored draft. Asks for guidance on student-startup process,
IP / COI considerations, advisor structure, and program fit. Does NOT
ask for funding in the first email._

Purdue Innovates is the umbrella organization at Purdue that handles
commercialization, IP licensing, and startup-formation support. They
also run the Foundry, the Ag-Celerator, and related programs. The
right first conversation with them is about process and fit, not
about capital.

---

## Primary draft

**To**: [Purdue Innovates intake — check current contact at purdueinnovates.org]
**Subject**: Purdue student team — guidance request on startup formation process

Dear [First name],

I'm leading a Purdue student team that has built HarvestMind, a
working diagnostic engine for perishable produce operators. The MVP
runs locally, a private demo URL is live, and the GitHub repo is
under my Purdue-affiliated account.

We are at the pre-customer-validation stage and trying to do this
correctly. Before we approach operators for paid engagements or
have any conversation about external capital, I'd value your
guidance on four process questions:

1. **Student-owned IP**. The codebase and product were developed
   outside of any Purdue-funded research project. We have not used
   Purdue research resources or grant-funded labs to build it.
   What is the recommended path to confirm clean student ownership
   before we sign any commercial contracts?

2. **Conflict of interest**. As we begin recruiting Purdue faculty
   as informal advisors, we want to make sure they disclose
   correctly through the proper COI process. What is the right
   timing — before any verbal agreement, or only when a formal
   advisor agreement is contemplated?

3. **Advisor structure**. We have not offered equity to anyone yet
   and we do not plan to without legal review and COI clearance.
   When the time comes, is there a Purdue-recommended advisor
   agreement template, or do you suggest founders use a standard
   external template (e.g. FAST)?

4. **Program fit**. The project is in the agrifood / supply-chain
   intelligence space. Are there active Purdue Innovates programs
   — Ag-Celerator, Foundry, related cohorts — where it would be
   appropriate to apply at this stage? Or should we wait until
   we have paid customers?

We are not asking for capital. We are asking for 25 minutes (or
even an email reply) on how to do the early steps correctly.

A one-page memo describing the project is available on request.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Your name]
Purdue [department], [year]
[Phone] · [Email]

## Why this email is structured this way

1. **Opens by demonstrating what we built**, then immediately
   pivots to process questions. This signals "we are not noise;
   we are a real project asking the right questions."

2. **Frames Purdue Innovates as a process resource**, not as a
   funding source. That matches what Purdue Innovates actually
   is at the early stage — most of their value at this point is
   IP clearance, COI guidance, and program awareness, not capital.

3. **Names the four most likely failure modes** for student
   startups at Purdue (IP ownership confusion, COI mistakes,
   bad advisor agreements, applying to the wrong program at
   the wrong time) and asks how to avoid them. This signals
   founder maturity in a way that opens doors.

4. **Asks for 25 minutes** but offers email reply as fallback.
   Purdue Innovates staff are often more responsive to specific
   email questions than to meeting requests at the first contact.

5. **No funding ask** in the first email. Asking too early is
   the most common reason these conversations stall — Purdue
   Innovates wants to see process maturity before introducing
   investor contacts.

## What to do after a positive reply

If they offer a meeting:

- Bring a one-page memo (printed, not just digital)
- Have written answers to "what would you ideally need from
  Purdue Innovates in the next 90 days" ready
- Do not pitch the product unless asked — let them lead

If they reply by email only:

- Save every answer in a tracking doc
- Re-engage in 60–90 days with new evidence (first paid
  pilot, first advisor confirmed, etc.)
- Reference their original answers in any follow-up — shows
  you treat them as a real source, not a checkbox

## What to do if they refer you elsewhere

Common likely referrals:

- **Purdue Research Foundation OTC** — for IP clearance
  specifically
- **The Foundry** — student venture support
- **Ag-Celerator** — agrifood program (relevant if it's
  running a current cohort)
- **Burton D. Morgan Center for Entrepreneurship** — general
  entrepreneurship resources

Each referral is a "yes, take this seriously" signal. Follow up
on every one within 5 business days.

## What never to include in the Purdue Innovates email

- A funding ask
- A pitch deck attachment
- A revenue projection
- A claim about "potential market size"
- The phrase "venture-scale"
- Any mention of valuation
- "Soon-to-be-incorporated" — unless you actually plan to file
  this quarter
- Equity grants you've already discussed verbally (don't bring
  that into the formal channel until it's been COI-cleared)

## Adjacent contacts worth approaching after Purdue Innovates

After the Purdue Innovates conversation, the natural next contacts
within the Purdue ecosystem are:

- **Burton D. Morgan Center for Entrepreneurship** — for
  entrepreneurship coursework, mentor matching
- **Discovery Park District / Convergence Center** — co-working
  and community
- **OTC** — Office of Technology Commercialization, for any
  IP question that Purdue Innovates passes to them
- **Faculty advisors** — Khir, Monaco, Fiechter, Deering, Torres
  (covered in `professor_outreach_emails.md`, Version A)

Sequencing matters: Purdue Innovates first, then OTC if they
refer you there, then faculty. Doing the order in reverse
sometimes creates COI friction later.

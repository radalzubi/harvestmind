# Purdue Advisor Equity — Internal Note

**Audience**: Team only. Not for sharing with advisors.
**Purpose**: Avoid mistakes that are common, costly, and very hard to unwind.

---

## The short version

**Do not offer equity casually.** Especially not in a first meeting. Especially
not to Purdue faculty or staff.

A Purdue professor saying "yes, I'd love to help" is not consent to be on
your cap table. Treating it as such will: damage the relationship, create
an IP-ownership dispute with the university, and lock you into a long-term
commitment with someone you barely know.

The order of operations is:

1. **First meeting** — ask for feedback, not commitment
2. **Second or third meeting** — gauge whether they're actually engaged
3. **Months later, if real ongoing contribution emerges** — discuss a formal
   advisor agreement through Purdue's proper channels
4. **Only after Purdue COI/IP clearance** — execute an advisor agreement
   with explicit terms, vesting, and time commitments

---

## Why Purdue is different from a generic advisor

Three Purdue-specific considerations that do not apply to private-sector advisors:

### 1. Intellectual property

If a Purdue professor contributes meaningfully to HarvestMind during their
Purdue-paid hours, **Purdue may have an IP claim on that contribution**.
This is not hypothetical. Universities routinely assert IP rights when
faculty contribute to commercial ventures without going through the proper
process. Purdue Research Foundation and the Office of Technology
Commercialization (OTC) are the relevant bodies.

The right path: before any structured advisor work, contact **Purdue OTC**
to confirm whether a license or assignment is needed and how to structure
the relationship to avoid IP conflict.

### 2. Conflict of interest

Purdue faculty must disclose outside consulting and equity holdings under
Purdue's COI policy. If you give a Purdue professor equity without their
COI office's awareness, you can expose them personally to disciplinary
action — and that is not a favor.

The right path: invite the professor to disclose the advisor role through
**Purdue's COI office** *before* equity is granted, not after.

### 3. Time

Purdue faculty have a 9-month or 12-month appointment with strict limits on
outside time. Many advisor agreements assume "a few hours per month" — that
may already exceed what their appointment permits. Do not put a professor
in the position of having to renegotiate their faculty appointment to honor
a commitment you asked for casually.

## Equity ranges (when the time comes)

These are starting points, not commitments. Apply only after a real
working relationship has been established and Purdue COI/IP processes
have been cleared.

| Advisor type | Typical equity range | Vesting | Time commitment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light advisor** | 0.10% – 0.25% | 2 years, monthly | 1–2 hours / quarter |
| **Active advisor** | 0.25% – 0.75% | 2 years, monthly | 2–4 hours / month, plus intros |
| **Strategic advisor** | 0.75% – 1.50% | 2–3 years, monthly | Material involvement: regular working sessions, deep customer intros, named on materials with permission |

**Notes**:

- All equity vests over time. **Cliff of at least 3 months.** Monthly vesting after.
- Equity grants always assume post-money valuation at time of grant. If you
  raise capital between now and the grant, the percentage may be on a
  different base.
- Equity is in common shares with single-trigger or double-trigger
  acceleration only on extraordinary events (acquisition, IPO). Not on
  graduation, sabbatical, or other ordinary life events.
- A signed Advisor Agreement is required. **Verbal agreement is not enough.**
  Use the FAST (Founder/Advisor Standard Template) form as a baseline.
- The agreement names: scope of work, time commitment, deliverables (if any),
  vesting schedule, termination clause, and confidentiality.

## The first meeting playbook

| Goal | Action |
|---|---|
| Get model feedback | Walk through the diagnostic. Ask specific questions. |
| Get pilot critique | Show the pilot offer. Ask if the structure is sane. |
| Get warm intros | Make a single specific ask: one operator who fits the ICP. |
| Test ongoing fit | Notice whether they bring up next steps unprompted. |

**Do not** in a first meeting:

- Bring up equity, advisor status, or compensation
- Send an advisor agreement
- Ask them to be on your website, deck, or marketing materials
- Imply they're a part of the team
- Send a thank-you email that says "looking forward to working together"
  (that phrase implies a commitment that has not been made)

The first thank-you email should say: "Thank you for the feedback. The most
useful pieces were X, Y, Z. If a fit operator comes to mind, we'd love to
hear it. Otherwise, I'll close the loop in a few weeks with what we
learned."

## When to actually offer an advisor role

Indicators that an advisor relationship is real, not aspirational:

- They have followed up with at least one substantive contribution between
  meetings (intro made, document reviewed unsolicited, problem flagged)
- They have re-engaged across at least 2 separate conversations without
  prompting from your side
- They have spent more than 90 minutes total on HarvestMind topics across
  the relationship
- They have either offered an intro or pressure-tested a specific decision
  in a way that materially changed your direction

Until 3 of 4 of those are true, you have a **friend of the project**, not
an advisor. Treat them well, but don't put them on a cap table.

---

## Process to follow before any equity grant to a Purdue person

1. **Email the professor** asking if they're open to a structured advisor
   role. Do not name equity numbers yet.
2. **They consult Purdue COI office.** Wait for their go-ahead.
3. **You consult Purdue OTC** about IP ownership of any HarvestMind work
   they may have already touched. Wait for clearance.
4. **Draft a FAST-style advisor agreement** with specific scope, vesting,
   and termination terms. Get a lawyer to review.
5. **Both parties sign.**
6. **Issue the equity grant** with a board consent (or sole-founder consent
   if pre-incorporation). File the 83(b) election within 30 days if
   applicable.
7. **Update the cap table** and store the executed agreement with corporate
   records.

If any of those steps feel like overkill in the moment, that is your
warning sign that you're moving too fast. The whole point of the process
is to protect both sides.

---

## Red flags from an advisor side

These are signs you should slow down or step back from an equity discussion:

- Advisor brings up equity unprompted in the first or second meeting
- Advisor asks for equity but cannot define what they will deliver
- Advisor asks for accelerated vesting on day one
- Advisor wants to be named on your deck, website, or marketing without a
  signed agreement
- Advisor expects equity in exchange for past help that was offered freely
- Advisor implies their network only opens up after equity is granted

If any of these appear, the relationship is transactional and the equity
will not buy you what you think it will. Decline politely and continue
without the formal relationship.

---

## Bottom line

The first meeting is about getting honest feedback. The fifth or sixth
meeting may be about a real advisor relationship. The space between is
where founders make expensive mistakes. Walk the process slowly. Document
everything. Run Purdue-specific steps before equity is mentioned.

When in doubt, get pressure-testing first and equity later.

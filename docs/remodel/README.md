# Commercial remodel and market verdict

**Verdict: MODIFY. Build a paid diagnostic and evidence-first replenishment
pilot—not a broad autonomous optimizer, and not a savings-guaranteed product.**

There is a real inventory-planning market, including documented deployments
in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Its core methods are not novel. HarvestMind's
potential advantage is an affordable, auditable route from an operator's
exports to a worthwhile purchasing change. That advantage remains a hypothesis
until somebody pays, supplies usable records and realizes repeatable net benefit.

## What this work delivers

- Corrected missing pipeline inputs and hardened selection/confirmation/live gates.
- Receipt-dated, causal synthetic replay; rejected earlier information-leaking
  runs are preserved and excluded from conclusions.
- Aggregate and per-period service-target candidates, kept experimental.
- Every run, adverse result and fallback in the [benchmark evidence](evidence/causal/).
- A 17-vendor feature audit, country-specific market analysis, pricing/ROI
  sensitivity, and a concrete paid-pilot design.

## The fifteen commercial answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Does a real market exist? | Yes. Named customers buy inventory/service planning; this does not establish unmet demand for HarvestMind. |
| 2. Are we reinventing the wheel? | Substantially, in forecasting, service constraints, stock right-sizing and optimization. These are necessary capabilities, not a moat. |
| 3. What is different? | Potentially transparent same-ledger policy testing, explicit abstention and low-commitment implementation. Exclusivity and willingness to pay are unproven. |
| 4. Why would a MENA distributor buy? | A trusted local implementation that improves a controllable purchasing decision using its own records, at a fee below verified benefit—not because “MENA is underserved.” |
| 5. Why would a US distributor buy? | A demonstrated gap in its configured ERP/heuristic workflow, with better net economics after integration/support cost. |
| 6. Why would they not buy? | Existing software is adequate; poor data; purchasing cannot change; benefits too small; fees/support too high; trust/security concerns; harmful recommendations. |
| 7. Which market first? | Jordan only with warm buyer/data access. Otherwise test UAE and US regional distributors in parallel; select on paid conversion and data readiness. |
| 8. Which customer first? | Stock-owning, roughly $20m–$100m distributor with one purchasing owner, exportable PO/receipt/stock history and sufficient controllable inventory/loss. Revenue band is a screening hypothesis. |
| 9. What charge? | Test $3k–$5k diagnostic. After proof, $12k–$25k annual offer or clearly defined 20% recurring-net-benefit share; no recurring charge on the same one-time cash release. |
| 10. First paid product? | Reconciled inventory, service and supplier diagnostic plus a short list of recommendations to test in shadow mode. |
| 11. Before selling what must exist? | Clear limited scope, secure read-only intake, reproducible ledger and pilot contract. Before selling savings: actual signed baseline, historical/shadow evidence and verified operating outcomes. |
| 12. Stop building what? | General ERP, generic risk-news dashboards, more exotic models, autonomous PO write-back, and investor TAM claims based on food throughput. |
| 13. Long-term moat? | Consented decision/outcome history, calibrated supplier/route records, repeatable integrations, references and delivery network. None is established merely by code. |
| 14. Venture-scale? | Unproven. At $25k ACV, $10m ARR needs 400 active clients and $100m needs 4,000. Qualified counts, repeatability, retention and margins have not been established. |
| 15. GO / MODIFY / PIVOT / STOP? | MODIFY. Go on a narrowly paid diagnostic; no-go on autonomous or guaranteed-savings commercialization. Stop expanding model scope until the first operator evidence. |

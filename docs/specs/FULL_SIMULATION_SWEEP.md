# Spec: Full Synthetic Simulation Sweep

_Written 2026-09-01, after the settlement correction (PR #9) merged into
`codex/commercial-pilot-readiness`._

## Where we are

The benchmark now replays supplier fills through one settlement function.
Current standing result on the Indiana generator, 5 seeds x 6 spot regimes,
`train_weeks=52`: **median +13.46% SP vs deterministic LP, gate 25/30**, all
five failures at 1.15x spot with 30% capacity.

The grid varies **one** thing: spot economics. That is not enough to know
where this model earns its keep.

## What to build

A scenario sweep that varies the parameters the thesis actually depends on,
reporting **every run individually**, not just medians.

### Grid A — the one that matters (full factorial)

**Supplier reliability x spot economics.** The whole premise is that value
comes from supply being unreliable. Nothing has tested that directly.

- mean supplier fill rate: **0.99, 0.95, 0.85, 0.70**
- spot regimes: the existing six (1.15/1.30/1.60 x 15%/30%)
- seeds: 5
- = 120 runs

**Hypothesis to test explicitly:** the SP edge shrinks toward zero as fill
rate approaches 0.99, and grows as it falls. If the edge does NOT decay at
0.99, something is wrong, because at that reliability there is almost no
supply uncertainty left to model. A confirmed decay curve is also the
quantitative basis for the segment argument: it says which industries this
is worth selling into.

### Grid B — one factor at a time, around a 0.85-fill / 1.30x / 30% baseline

1. **Correlated supplier shortfall.** Currently every supplier's yield is
   drawn independently via per-supplier `beta.rvs`. The copula layer in
   `risk/correlated_scenarios.py` is NOT under test. Wire it in and sweep
   Kendall tau: **0.0, 0.3, 0.6**. This is the differentiated claim and it
   has never been benchmarked.
2. **Demand intermittency.** Fraction of product-weeks with zero demand:
   **0%, 20%, 50%**. Exercises Croston routing and the zero-demand
   settlement path fixed in PR #9, which is currently inert on this
   generator.
3. **Cost asymmetry.** Underage:overage ratio **1:1, 3:1, 9:1**. Drives the
   critical fractile; the SP should gain as asymmetry rises.
4. **Operator scale.** Annualised throughput **$6M, $15M, $50M**, matching
   the ICP band question.
5. **Training history.** `train_weeks` **26, 52, 78** — how much history an
   operator must supply before this works.

## Methodology requirements

These are not style preferences. Each one is a mistake already made in this
repo.

- **Conventional median** (`statistics.median`). Not `sorted(x)[n//2]`.
- **Report every run**, with seed and parameters. A median hid a sign flip
  once already.
- **Include a `force_unit_fills=True` control** in at least one cell as a
  regression check, and confirm it cannot open the gate.
- **No leakage.** Any fallback must use training-window data only. Realized
  fills settle decisions; they must never reach a policy at decision time.
- **All policies through one settlement function.** No policy-specific
  scoring paths.
- **State assumptions inline.** The pro-rata supplier allocation for
  supplier-blind policies is an assumption and affects their scores.

## Reporting requirements

1. A results table with one row per run.
2. The **fill-rate decay curve** as the headline: SP edge vs mean fill rate,
   holding spot fixed. This is the most decision-relevant output.
3. Gate pass rate per cell, not only overall.
4. An explicit statement of which cells FAIL and why, in economic terms.
5. Absolute costs alongside percentage edges.

## What not to claim

- Nothing here is validation. It is synthetic data throughout, generated by
  us, and must be labelled that way in every artifact.
- Do not claim value from correlated shortfall unless Grid B.1 is actually
  wired to `risk/correlated_scenarios.py` and shows it.
- Do not report a favourable median without the range and the failures.
- If a result looks surprisingly good, suspect the harness first. Two large
  results in this repo were harness defects, not model performance.

## Deliverable

A branch off `codex/commercial-pilot-readiness`, with the sweep script, the
results as machine-readable output, a report, and a clear statement of
which regions of the parameter space the model earns its complexity in and
which it does not.

# Commercial questions — evidence, not feature count

**NEEDS FURTHER ENGINEERING.** This is a real network optimizer implementation,
not an enterprise-ready or real-customer-validated product. Percentages below
refer to modeled finite-horizon economic cost, never customer revenue.

1. **Can ZAAD beat competent normal planning where there is opportunity?**
   Conditionally, in this synthetic coverage experiment: gated normal median
   +1.71% vs projected base-stock, range−7.62% to+14.18%,11cost losses/295pairs.
   Service breaches remain180/295 versus base-stock183/295. Broad same-or-better
   service and economic release criteria are not established.
2. **When does base-stock remain sufficient?** Retain it when a candidate fails
   independent benefit or stress confirmation. The current controller makes
   small incremental improvements; it does not justify replacing a competent
   incumbent everywhere. No real industry segmentation threshold is measured by
   this experiment, and the proxy is not the customer's actual recorded policy.
3. **Does MEIO materially reduce inventory?** Not established independently.
   Age-indexed central/branch inventory and transfers are live, but there is no
   separate standard-MEIO comparator or isolated inventory-reduction effect.
4. **Do transfers create value?** Yes, conditionally: five matched six-period
   fixtures show median$245.10 lower modeled cost, range$137.17–$306.21,0/5losses.
   This is the same effect as placement in that probe, not two additive gains.
5. **Does substitution materially protect service?** Not shown here. It lowers
   modeled cost by median$185.46, range$147.14–$208.79,0/5cost losses, but ties
   service in three pairs and slightly reduces it in two. Approved equivalents
   can expand feasibility; these results do not establish a service uplift.
6. **Do cancellation/de-expediting improve recovery economics?** In explicit
   contract-permitted fixtures: cancellation$114.50 in every pair; de-expediting
   median$130.03, range$123.28–$134.76,0/5losses. Contract terms are constructed
   assumptions. This is not evidence every supplier offers those rights.
7. **Does cash optimization alter orders?** Cash, credit, deposits, LC and dated
   payments directly constrain purchase/PO decisions; cash stress causes real
   infeasibility/abstention, not an invented affordable plan. A paired value
   ablation remains missing, and post-horizon invoice financeability is not
   certified. Do not sell full affordability assurance yet.
8. **Does customer-specific service improve allocation?** Priority/channel and
   declared-line constraints are implemented and tested. Incremental commercial
   value is not isolated; aggregate synthetic demand cannot establish enterprise
   multi-line OTIF performance.
9. **Does supplier/route diversification improve resilience?** Actual source
   quantities, path/shared-resource limits and shared failures are represented.
   A separate paired supplier-allocation effect is not measured; the current
   rotated-topology matrix cannot attribute all robust gains to diversification.
10. **Does censored ETA improve decisions?** It handles open shipments and
    unresolved tails rather than dropping them from the risk set. Decision-value
    ablation and real calibration are still missing. Sparse history remains
    explicitly quote-based/unsupported, not falsely confident P90 timing.
11. **Does dynamic forecasting fix structural shifts?** It helps increases:
    stochastic vs frozen median+13.85%, range+3.02%–+27.44%,0/20losses. It does
    **not fix decreases**: median+0.23%, range−30.96%–+9.73%,9/20losses. Every-
    review refits, CUSUM and known-promotion events are live, but "shift failure
    solved" would be false.
12. **Does robust/DRO planning reduce misspecification harm?** The implemented
    **stress-envelope** optimizer does; it is not claimed to be calibrated DRO.
    Against stochastic planning,25matched misspecification pairs have median
    +28.37% cost saving, range+16.96%–+39.07%,0losses. Service breaches decrease
    from25/25 to15/25, still far from a guarantee. The gated controller retains
    a failing incumbent in these states and still breaches25/25. Candidate value
    and controller effectiveness are different questions.
13. **What adds no measured value?** Standalone MEIO, censored ETA, customer
    segmentation, cash constraints and source-allocation incremental dollars
    remain unmeasured, not proved worthless. Continuous quantities lose on one
    held-out probe. News timing/paid signals and downside-shift adaptation do not
    support a positive blanket commercial claim.
14. **What prevents real deployment?** The reproduced numerical-dust PO failure;
    financial tail; service/downshift robustness; incomplete multi-line order
    reconciliation; unqualified dense stochastic job scaling; and inherited
    static security findings. Exact reproductions and next acceptance checks
    are in [failure analysis](FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md). Real prospective customer
    reconciliation remains essential, but it is not the only missing step.

## What was actually built

Sparse scenario-based network MPC with continuous quantities and integer packs,
not a finite buffer-policy menu. Supplier/route/location/time orders, age-indexed
inventory, transfers, approved substitution and contractual cancel/reschedule/
expedite/de-expedite options share resource and dated liquidity constraints.
Conditional Kaplan–Meier handles censored ETA; adaptive forecast tournaments
refit at every review. A stress-optimized portfolio is checked against an
incumbent with independent confirmation and adverse assumptions. Authenticated
SQLite shadow decisions use version/hash/expiry review, without PO execution.

The most defensible next commercial step is **resolve the named blockers, then
run a narrowly scoped prospective shadow comparison against an actual buyer**.
Do not price gainshare from these synthetic percentages or claim Oracle parity.
